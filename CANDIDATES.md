# Candidate forecasts — Funniest Forecast for a City That Isn't

Brief (judge, in priority order): (1) a weather forecast for a city that
does not exist; (2) funny — the laugh comes from the weather itself, not a
bolted-on pun; (3) sounds like a flat official bulletin, then breaks;
(4) the invented city feels like a real place, name + detail fitting its
absurd weather; (5) at most 120 characters, no wasted word.

All ten lines below are original, written for this entry. None is copied
from a meme, a joke book, or another entry.

## Candidates

1. Drizzleford: rain falling sideways since Tuesday. Expect continued
   sideways rain, turning upward by Friday.
2. Glimmerwick: sunny, with a 90% chance of yesterday. Bring tomorrow's
   umbrella; today has already happened.
3. Bumblehatch: fog so thick the town is officially missing. Search
   parties resume when visibility returns.
4. Crumbletown: scattered showers of breadcrumbs, then a 40% chance of
   pigeons. Picnics cancelled until further notice.
5. Natterford: winds of 40 mph carrying gossip eastward. Residents
   should secure loose secrets before noon.
6. Fizzlewick: thunderstorms of applause expected after 6pm. The mayor
   takes full credit for the lightning.
7. Muddle-on-Sea: high tide of custard at noon. Lifeguards advise
   swimming with a spoon.
8. Grumblestowe: overcast, with a chance of Mondays. The sun has filed
   a formal complaint and left the county.
9. Wobbleton: light drizzle of left socks today, heavy by evening.
   Umbrellas useless; laundry baskets advised.
10. Hushbury: silence falling steadily all week, with a 100% chance of
    someone saying "did you hear that?" by dawn.

## Evaluation

- #5 and #6 lean on a pun / a bolted-on gag (gossip winds, the mayor
  taking credit) — the judge's criterion 2 punishes exactly that.
- #2 ("90% chance of yesterday") and #8 ("chance of Mondays") reuse
  joke shapes that already circulate widely; they risk reading as
  familiar rather than original.
- #4 is a good chain (breadcrumbs, then pigeons) but the laugh lands on
  the pigeons, not on the weather.
- #10 inverts nicely, but "did you hear that?" is a generic punchline
  and the weather (silence) is barely weather.
- #3 is strong: fog so thick the town is officially missing, with a
  flat official remedy. The laugh, though, is about the town, not the
  fog itself.
- #7 is strong: a custard tide fits a seaside town called Muddle-on-Sea,
  and "swimming with a spoon" is a deadpan advisory. But custard is a
  food joke wearing a weather coat.
- #9 is strong: sock rain is pure weather absurdity with a deadpan
  advisory ("laundry baskets advised"). The image is random rather than
  escalating, though.
- #1 is the best fit: the weather itself is the joke (rain that falls
  sideways, then turns upward), the bulletin stays flat throughout,
  "Drizzleford" is a place name that fits endless rain, and the line
  escalates — sideways since Tuesday, upward by Friday. No pun, no
  borrowed joke shape, no wasted word.

## Pick

Candidate #1:

Drizzleford: rain falling sideways since Tuesday. Expect continued sideways rain, turning upward by Friday.
